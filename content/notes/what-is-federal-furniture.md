Federal style furniture refers to an American decorative arts movement that emerged around 1780 to 1830, following the American Revolution and during the early Federalist era. [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_furniture), [2](https://www.laurelcrown.com/reproduction-furniture-styles/know-your-antique-styles-federal-furniture), [3](https://pearlcityantiques.com/a/blog/post/federal-period-1780-to-1820)

## Key characteristics

- **Neoclassical influence:** Inspired by ancient Greek and Roman design, heavily adapted from English designers like George Hepplewhite and Thomas Sheraton, as well as architect Robert Adam.
- **Lighter proportions:** Straight, slender, tapered or turned legs — away from Chippendale and Queen Anne cabriole legs.
- **Symmetry and geometry:** Rectilinear or bow-front / serpentine case pieces with strict vertical and horizontal balance.
- **Wood and inlay:** Mahogany, cherry, or walnut with contrasting light-wood veneers, stringing, and geometric paterae.
- **Motifs:** Eagle, bellflower (husk) chains, urns, and swags.

## Notable makers

- Duncan Phyfe
- John and Thomas Seymour
- Charles-Honoré Lannuier
