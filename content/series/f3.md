[F3](https://github.com/future-file-format/F3) is a research columnar file format. The SIGMOD 2025 paper argues for a different split than Parquet: a stable way to find bytes in the file, and the option to ship a decoder with an unfamiliar encoding. Upstream says not to use it in production. The Elixir library in [github.com/thanos/f3](https://github.com/thanos/f3) is an experiment on top of one pinned upstream commit.

**Library:** [github.com/thanos/f3](https://github.com/thanos/f3) · **Upstream:** [future-file-format/F3](https://github.com/future-file-format/F3) · **Paper:** [doi:10.1145/3749163](https://doi.org/10.1145/3749163)

Parts 1–2 explain the idea and the byte layout. Parts 3–5 open, stream, and write files from Elixir when the native decoder is compiled. Part 6 is Wasm (opt-in only). Part 7 places F3 next to Arrow and Parquet as jobs, not as a speed contest. Parts 8–9 are the safety rules and a recorded bench run that refuses to invent decode numbers.

Hex has not published the package. Wire version 0.1 only. Wasm stays off unless a read passes `wasm: :sandbox`. If an article and the repository disagree, trust the repository.
